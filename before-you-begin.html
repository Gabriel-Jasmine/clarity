How Clarity works

About

Big life decisions call for the right questions and mental models to help you see as much of the full picture as you can before making a well-informed decision.

Clarity guides you through 10 stages of reflection built around Opportunity Cost, Incentives, Compounding, Second-order Thinking, Inversion, Margin of Safety, and Reversibility. It also includes a dedicated section to challenge your thinking against psychological biases that can quietly influence your judgment - here, we call them the ghosts.

Through a series of guided questions, Clarity helps you look closer at what matters to you, what drives your decision, what you might be overlooking, and the consequences that could follow.

How to make the most of Clarity

  • Start with one decision you're trying to make. Focus on the action you're considering and what you hope to achieve. You'll explore alternatives along the way.
  • For open-ended questions, refer to the Example and Thinking Guide, where available, to understand how to approach the question and what you could consider in your answer.
  • Remember, these are only guides. Your answers should reflect your own thoughts, not what the examples suggest. Be as honest and specific as you can, including when you don't know something yet.
  • You can move between sections and revisit earlier answers as your thinking develops.

What to expect

At the end, your answers come together in a Decision Overview, bringing your reasons, trade-offs, possible consequences, and unanswered questions into one place.

Clarity won't tell you what to do. It helps you see your own thinking more clearly, including what you might have overlooked and where your judgment could be influenced by the ghosts.

Remember, the overview can only be as helpful as the honesty and depth of your answers. The more thoughtfully you reflect, the more useful it becomes in helping you make a well-informed decision.

So, take as much time as you need.
